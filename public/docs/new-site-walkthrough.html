Launch a new site, step by step

The whole job in order, from an empty screen to a site you would send to members: design, pages, images, the calendar, members and payments. Every button name below is the one on the screen.

Before you start

Gather these first and the rest takes about an hour.

You needNotes
Guild nameAs you want it read on the site.
Web addressLowercase letters, numbers and hyphens. It cannot be changed afterwards — it becomes quilthosting.com/g/your-address and your-address.quilthosting.com.
City and meeting scheduleOne line each, e.g. "Austin, Texas" and "Second Tuesday, 6:30 pm, Grace Church hall". These fill in real sentences on the starter pages.
LogoPNG or JPEG. Any size — it is resized for you. A wide wordmark reads better in a header than a tall badge.
Six to ten photosQuilts, a meeting, the room. Photos are what make the site look like a real guild rather than a template.

1. Create the guild

Sign in at /admin with Google, then Create a guild. The wizard has four screens:

  1. Tell us about your guild — name, web address, city, meeting schedule.
  2. Pick a design — six designs to start from; there are many more later.
  3. Make it yours — upload the logo, and take colours from it if you like.
  4. You're live — the site exists, with pages already written.

You can leave at any point with Save and finish later; nothing is lost. The new guild starts with a working set of pages, each paragraph beginning "Sample text — replace me:" so you can see what still needs your words.

2. Set the time zone

Do this before you add events. Go to Settings → Guild → Time zone, choose your zone, and press Save time zone.

A guild created through the wizard takes the time zone of the computer that created it, so this is usually already right. Check it anyway: it decides what time your members read on the website. You always enter times in your own clock; this setting is how they are shown to everyone else.

3. Choose a design

Everything visual lives in one place: Website (in the sidebar under SITE), then scroll to Design.

  • Browse designs — each card is a small picture of the page in that design. Click the picture to see it full size with your guild's own name and events in it, then Use this design from the preview, or from the card.
  • Colors — palettes grouped by family (Heritage, Modern, Black & white, Naturals, Jewel, Soft, Seasonal, Dark). Again, click the picture to see it full size. The Readability check underneath shows real contrast numbers; every palette passes before it is offered.
  • Page tone — Paper, Cream, Tinted or Deep: how much colour the page itself carries. The same palette can read as crisp and white or as a full colour page. This is the fastest way to make your site look unlike anyone else's.
  • Type — a display font for headings and a body font, plus the type size.
  • Shape and spacing, Header, Footer, Pattern — corners and shadows, how much room sections get, where the logo and menu sit, and an optional quilt-block pattern behind sections.

Press Save design when you are happy. Changing the design never changes your words — the pages and every sentence on them are kept.

4. Edit the pages

Website → Pages lists what you have. Each row has Edit, Preview draft and Delete. New page adds one.

Inside the editor:

  • The page is a stack of sections. Click one to select it; the panel on the right has a Content tab (the words) and a Style tab (background, width, spacing, alignment, which side the picture sits on).
  • Add a section lists everything available, grouped: Openers (Hero), Content (Text, Image, Feature grid, Questions & answers, Timeline, Pull quote), Membership (Membership levels, Join band, Meeting info, Officers & board, Benefits), Events (Events, Featured event), Community (Testimonials, Photo gallery, News & newsletter, Projects, Sponsors), Business and Utility.
  • Sections with several looks say so — Hero · 5 layouts offers Photo (full width), Split — photo beside text, Quilt pattern band, Minimal, and Numbers strip.
  • Your work saves as a draft as you type. The site does not change until you Publish. Preview shows the draft; Discard draft throws it away; History restores an earlier version; Unpublish takes a page off the site without deleting it.

Work through every page and replace the "Sample text — replace me" paragraphs. That sentence is the only thing standing between a starter site and a real one.

5. Images

Adding a picture

  1. Select the section, open the Style tab (or Content for a gallery or a card).
  2. Upload image takes one from your computer; Choose from library reuses one you already uploaded.
  3. Focal point sets what stays in frame when the picture is cropped — put it on a face or the centre of the quilt. There are 16:9 and 1:1 previews so you can see the crop.
  4. Remove takes the picture out of the section. The file stays in your library for use elsewhere.

What to upload

QuestionAnswer
FormatsJPEG, PNG or WebP. GIFs work but keep their animation only at full size.
Size limit10 MB per image.
How big should it be?About 2400 px on the long edge is plenty. Bigger is not better — it is just slower to upload.
Do I need to shrink it myself?No. Every upload is stored at five widths (240, 480, 960, 1600, 2400) in two modern formats, and each visitor's browser is sent the smallest one that still looks sharp on their screen.
Photos of peopleAsk before publishing. Guild members should know their picture is on a public page.

Replacing and deleting

  • Replace: select the section and upload a new one. The old file stays in your library.
  • Delete for good: Files in the sidebar lists every upload with Download and Delete. Deleting a file that a page still uses leaves a gap on that page, so remove it from the page first.

6. The logo

Settings → Guild → Upload logo. It appears in the header of every page and in search results. It is resized on upload, so a large file is fine; the header shows it about 44 px tall, so a wide wordmark reads better than a tall badge.

7. Events and the calendar

Adding an event

Events → Add Event. The fields are:

FieldNotes
Title, Description, LocationWhat members read.
Start, End (optional)In your clock. Shown to members in the guild's time zone (step 2).
CapacityBlank means unlimited.
Member price / Non-member priceLeave both at zero for a free meeting.
Enable waitlist when fullTakes names once capacity is reached.
RepeatsFor monthly meetings, bees and workshops: choose the pattern, how many occurrences, which week and which weekday, and the whole series is created at once.
+ Add questionAsks registrants something extra — machine brought, dietary needs, skill level.

The Events screen itself has List and Calendar views, and each event has Edit, Close reg (stop taking registrations) and Delete.

Where events appear on the site

Two ready-made pages exist as soon as you have an event, with no setup:

  • Your address + /events — the list.
  • Your address + /calendar — a month grid.

As soon as the guild has an upcoming public event, an Events link is added to the site menu automatically. If you would rather it sat somewhere else, or read something else, edit the menu yourself in the website builder — once you set your own menu, it is yours and nothing is added to it.

Putting a calendar on a page

To show events on the home page or any other page: open the page, Add a section, and under Events choose Events — it has four layouts, including a month calendar grid, cards, a list, and Featured event for a single next-up event. Publish the page.

8. Levels and members

  1. Levels — add at least one membership level with a name and a price. This is what "Join" leads to. A level can run from the join date, over a calendar year, or from a fixed date, with an optional grace period; households let one payment cover several people.
  2. Members → Add Member for one, or Import CSV for a roster you already have. Export CSV takes it back out again at any time.
  3. Members sign in at the member portal with an emailed link — there is no password for them to forget.

9. Payments

Settings → Payments connects Stripe so dues and event fees reach the guild's own bank account. Until it is connected, joins and paid registrations cannot complete. Stripe asks for the guild's details directly; QuiltHosting never sees bank numbers.

10. Your address

Every guild gets quilthosting.com/g/your-address and a free subdomain, your-address.quilthosting.com. The dashboard shows the subdomain's status while it is being set up. A guild that owns a domain can use it instead — see Domains.

11. Going live

The dashboard's Get your guild ready checklist counts itself from your real data — nothing is ticked by hand — and each unfinished step has a do this link straight to the screen that finishes it. Before you send the address to members, walk the site once as a visitor:

  • No "Sample text — replace me" left anywhere.
  • The logo, guild name and meeting details are right.
  • Join leads to a level with a price, and payments are connected.
  • An event exists, and its time reads correctly on /events.
  • Open the site on a phone. Every page is built to work there; look anyway.

Removing things

To removeWhere
A sectionSelect it in the page editor and delete it, then Publish.
A picture from a sectionRemove in the image controls. The file stays in your library.
An uploaded file for goodFiles → Delete. Take it off any page first.
A pageWebsite → Delete. It goes to Trash, where it can be restored. Links to the old address keep working.
A page, but only from the menuTurn off "show in menu" for that page, or edit the menu.
A page from the public site without deleting itUnpublish in the editor.
An eventEvents → Delete. To keep it visible but stop sign-ups, use Close reg.
The Events link in the menuSet your own menu in the website builder; your list is never added to.

When something looks wrong

What you seeWhat it is
Event times are hours offThe guild's time zone is not set. Settings → Guild → Time zone. You enter times in your own clock; this decides how they are shown.
An event exists but the site does not show itThe /events and /calendar pages always have it. To show it inside a page, add an Events section to that page and publish.
Your edit is not on the siteEdits are drafts until you Publish. Preview draft shows what you have so far.
A picture looks soft or oddly croppedUpload a larger original (about 2400 px on the long edge) and set the Focal point.
The site looks like everyone else'sChange Page tone, then the palette. Then put your own photographs in — that is the difference between a template and a guild's site.
